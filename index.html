Abstract

Rebar installation — placing steel reinforcement bars into support racks before concrete is poured — is among the most repetitive and physically demanding tasks on construction sites. Automating it means contact-rich, millimeter-clearance insertion of parts that, unlike machined components, differ from bar to bar within production tolerances. Learned insertion systems today rely on real-world data, which is costly for heavy steel parts and does not track the geometry of each new batch.

We build and deploy GRIT, a visual sim-to-real system trained entirely in simulation: a privileged state-based teacher is trained with reinforcement learning over procedurally generated rebar and rack geometries, and distilled into a multi-view visual student that acts directly on raw RGB, trained under heavy appearance randomization and with dynamics randomization anchored at system-identified values. Deployment therefore carries no perception stack at all — no segmentation model, no pose estimator, only calibrated cameras and the policy.

Without any real-world data or fine-tuning, GRIT exceeds 95 % insertion success on hardware across bars spanning one design's tolerance range, and covers a nine-design catalog in simulation — a level of zero-shot transfer rarely, if ever, shown by an end-to-end visual insertion policy, since prior sim-to-real insertion systems act on state from a separate pose estimator. Ablations show that training-time geometry randomization is decisive across production tolerances and speeds adaptation to unseen designs.

Background

On site, workers lift each bar, align it with the slots of a support rack, and force it down — thousands of times per site. The motion is a classic contact-rich insertion problem, with one complication that laboratory peg-in-hole settings do not have: the parts themselves vary. Fabricated rebar carries production tolerances in bend angle, cut length, out-of-plane twist, and hook geometry, so a practical system has to be robust to a distribution of geometries rather than one CAD model.

Training Framework

Three phases, one controller. A privileged teacher is trained with RL on procedurally generated geometry; it is distilled into a multi-view visual student under heavy appearance and dynamics randomization; the student is then deployed zero-shot, weights frozen, through the identical task-space impedance controller.

Phase 1

Privileged teacher — RL in simulation

State-based policy over privileged proprioception, object and contact state. Trained across procedurally generated rebar and rack geometries, with reset-state banks covering free-space approach and partially inserted states.

Phase 2

Visual student — distillation

Shared ResNet-18 encoder over multiple calibrated views plus sim-to-real proprioception. DAgger + behavior cloning (β = 0.5) under heavy appearance randomization and system-identified dynamics randomization.

Phase 3

Zero-shot deployment

Frozen weights, same controller, same camera layout. No segmentation model, no pose estimator, no fine-tuning — and no real-world data anywhere in the pipeline.
